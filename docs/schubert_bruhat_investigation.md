# Schubert/Bruhat investigation for fermionic carrier geometry

## Verdict

The direct proposal—

> every determinant carrier is a Schubert cell, Richardson variety, or a union
> governed by Bruhat order—

is false.

The Schubert route remains useful only on a special **decomposable/matroidal
subsector**.  The correct universal geometry of an arbitrary carrier is instead
the toric geometry of its support polytope, together with additional
non-toric phase directions.  This produces a general invariant that directly
explains several empirical features of the project.

## 1. Why the direct Grassmannian identification fails

The projective pure-state space for \(N\) fermions in \(d\) orbitals is

\[
\mathbb P(\wedge^N\mathbb C^d).
\]

The Grassmannian

\[
\operatorname{Gr}(N,d)\hookrightarrow
\mathbb P(\wedge^N\mathbb C^d)
\]

is only the Plücker variety of decomposable wedges, i.e. single Slater
determinants in arbitrary orbital frames.  A generic correlated superposition
of determinant basis states violates the Plücker relations and lies outside
the Grassmannian.

A carrier \(S\) of \(m\) determinants therefore defines the coordinate linear
space

\[
L_S=\mathbb P\operatorname{span}\{|D\rangle:D\in S\}
\cong\mathbb P^{m-1},
\]

not a Schubert variety in general.

Its intersection \(L_S\cap\operatorname{Gr}(N,d)\) can be interesting, but it
describes only decomposable states inside the carrier, not the carrier's
generic correlated states or its full 2-RDM observable manifold.

## 2. Exact matroid obstruction

A support \(S\subseteq\binom{[d]}N\) can occur as the set of nonzero Plücker
coordinates of a Grassmannian point only if \(S\) is the set of bases of a
realizable rank-\(N\) matroid.  In particular it must satisfy basis exchange.

The cyclic-window family fails this test almost everywhere.  Exact enumeration
through \(d=14\) gives:

- \(N=1\) and \(N=d-1\): matroidal trivial edge cases;
- \((d,N)=(4,2)\): the only nontrivial internal cyclic case found;
- every cyclic family with \(d\ge5\) and \(1<N<d-1\): not a matroid basis set.

Therefore the project's flagship coprime cyclic families usually do not even
define a Grassmannian matroid stratum.  Schubert calculus cannot be their
universal language.

## 3. Bruhat order is also too restrictive

For the standard flag, torus-fixed points of a Grassmannian Schubert variety
are indexed by an order ideal in componentwise (Gale/Bruhat) order on
\(N\)-subsets.

The cyclic-window supports are not such order ideals except trivial cases.
Their cyclic symmetry is incompatible with the preferred linear flag order.
Changing the flag may move the Schubert variety, but it does not turn an
arbitrary coordinate support into a Schubert fixed-point ideal.

Thus the observed amplitude-support boundary lattice is not generally the
Bruhat poset.

## 4. Universal replacement: the support toric variety

Let

\[
S=\{D_1,\ldots,D_m\},\qquad
\chi_D\in\{0,1\}^d
\]

be determinant occupation vectors.  The diagonal complex orbital torus
\((\mathbb C^\*)^d/\mathbb C^\*\) acts on
\(\mathbb P(\wedge^N\mathbb C^d)\).  For any full-support state in \(L_S\), the
normalization of its torus-orbit closure is the projective toric variety
associated with

\[
P_S=\operatorname{conv}\{\chi_D:D\in S\}.
\]

The diagonal one-body moment map is

\[
\mu_{\mathrm{diag}}([\psi])
 = \sum_{D\in S}|c_D|^2\chi_D,
\]

so its image over the full carrier is precisely \(P_S\).

This construction requires no Plücker relations and therefore applies to every
carrier, including non-matroidal cyclic supports.

## 5. New invariant: non-toric phase complexity

Let \(A_1\) be the \(d\times m\) singleton-incidence matrix whose columns are
the occupation vectors \(\chi_D\).  Since every column sums to \(N\),

\[
\dim P_S=\operatorname{rank}A_1-1.
\]

The full carrier has \(m-1\) independent relative phase directions, while the
diagonal orbital torus can generate only \(\dim P_S\) of them.  Define

\[
\boxed{\quad
\kappa(S)
  =(m-1)-\dim P_S
  =m-\operatorname{rank}A_1.
\quad}
\]

Interpretation:

- \(\kappa(S)=0\): every carrier-relative phase can be generated by diagonal
  one-body orbital phases; the support exponent vectors are affinely
  independent and \(P_S\) is a simplex.
- \(\kappa(S)>0\): the carrier contains intrinsic many-determinant interference
  phases invisible to the diagonal one-body torus.

This invariant is exactly the nullity of singleton incidence.

## 6. Cyclic-window theorem

For the cyclic \(N\)-window carrier on \(d\) orbitals, the already derived
incidence rank formula gives

\[
\operatorname{rank}A_1=d-\gcd(d,N)+1.
\]

Because the carrier contains \(m=d\) determinants,

\[
\boxed{\quad
\kappa(C_{d,N})=\gcd(d,N)-1.
\quad}
\]

Consequences:

1. Coprime cyclic windows have \(\kappa=0\).  Their support polytopes are
   simplices, and all relative phases are orbital-toric.
2. Non-coprime windows have exactly \(\gcd(d,N)-1\) intrinsic interference
   phase directions.
3. The same arithmetic condition that made singleton incidence invertible now
   has a geometric meaning: it is precisely the condition for zero
   non-toric phase complexity.

This is a reusable general insight, not merely a failed Schubert analogy.

## 7. Relation to observable reconstruction

The 2-RDM coherence graph reconstructs phase directions that the diagonal
1-RDM moment map cannot see.

The phase space naturally separates into:

- \(\operatorname{rank}A_1-1\) orbital-toric phases;
- \(\kappa(S)\) internal interference phases.

This suggests refining every carrier audit by reporting:

1. support-polytope dimension;
2. non-toric phase complexity \(\kappa\);
3. the minimum RDM order needed to recover the internal phases;
4. whether those internal directions are linearly visible or require
   rank-one multiplicative closure.

The 28 order-two exceptions looked like natural candidates for an obstruction
concentrated in the internal phase sector rather than in weight recovery. The
census settles it the other way. Every one of the 28 has `kappa = 0`, so their
support polytopes are simplices with no internal phase sector at all, while all
63 positive-`kappa` records reconstruct at order two.

The association needs one caveat before it is read as a law. Positive `kappa`
first becomes possible at support size 6, and 26 of the 28 failures sit at
sizes 2 to 4 where `kappa = 0` is forced. Restricting to sizes 6 through 9,
where both classes occur, gives 0 failures among 58 positive-`kappa` carriers
against 2 among 356 simplex carriers, which is too small to be decisive. What
is safe to say is that intrinsic phase complexity is not the obstruction: the
order-two failures are small carriers whose determinants are too far apart in
excitation distance for a two-body channel to connect them.

## 8. Where Schubert calculus remains viable

Schubert methods remain useful in three narrower settings.

### 8.1 Deriving generalized Pauli inequalities

The global one-body spectral polytope is already governed by
representation-theoretic and Schubert-calculus machinery.  This is the proper
role of Schubert calculus in the original GPC problem.

### 8.2 Matroidal carrier intersections

When \(S\) is the basis set of a realizable matroid,
\(L_S\cap\operatorname{Gr}(N,d)\) contains a matroid stratum.  Its torus-orbit
closure has matroid polytope \(P_S\), and its Chow or \(K\)-class can be
expanded in the Schubert basis.

This can assign established algebraic invariants to the decomposable locus of
selected carriers, but does not classify generic correlated states in \(L_S\).

### 8.3 Schubert-compatible supports

If a support is an order ideal under Gale order, Schubert fixed-point
combinatorics can organize the decomposable boundary.  This should be treated
as a special carrier class, not a universal principle.

## 9. Product and gluing warning

For supports \(S_1,S_2\) on disjoint orbital blocks, the support polytope of
the Cartesian product is

\[
P_{S_1\times S_2}=P_{S_1}\times P_{S_2}.
\]

But the full glued carrier is

\[
\mathbb P^{m_1m_2-1},
\]

whereas the orbital toric orbit has dimension
\(\dim P_{S_1}+\dim P_{S_2}\).  Hence the full glued carrier generally has a
large internal phase complexity

\[
\kappa(S_1\times S_2)
 =m_1m_2-\operatorname{rank}A_{1,\mathrm{product}}.
\]

The toric product describes the orbital-phase orbit, not every entangled state
in the full glued carrier.  Any scalable product-manifold benchmark must state
whether it propagates the Segre/product locus or the full carrier.

This is not hypothetical. Gluing two coprime cyclic factors, each a support
simplex with `kappa = 0`, gives `kappa = 16` for `C_{5,2}` glued with itself
(25 determinants, singleton rank 9) and `kappa = 24` for `C_{5,2}` with
`C_{7,3}`. And `docs/generalized_observable_benchmark.md` propagates the
product locus: its Hamiltonian has no inter-cell coupling and the Schmidt rank
stays 1 at every tested size, so the state never leaves the Segre variety. Its
`O(q)` versus `O(q 2^q)` separation is a statement about that locus, which is
exactly the distinction this section asks benchmarks to make.

## 10. Final assessment

### Disproved

- arbitrary carriers are Schubert or Richardson varieties;
- carrier boundary incidence is generally Bruhat order;
- cyclic-window families are generally matroid strata of a Grassmannian.

### Viable and generalized

- every carrier has a canonical support polytope and toric orbital-phase
  geometry;
- singleton-incidence nullity is a geometric phase-complexity invariant;
- cyclic windows satisfy the exact formula
  \(\kappa=\gcd(d,N)-1\);
- Schubert classes remain meaningful for the realizable matroidal/decomposable
  subsector and for the global GPC inequalities.

The fruitful next direction is therefore **toric support geometry and internal
phase complexity**, with Schubert calculus used selectively rather than as the
universal framework.
