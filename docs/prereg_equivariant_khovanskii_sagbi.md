## The exact algebraic target

ONE target, the preferred one in the brief, and the fallback is not used.

Let `V = wedge^n C^d` and let `U` be the maximal unipotent subgroup of `GL(d)`
of upper unitriangular matrices. The target is the HIGHEST-WEIGHT ALGEBRA

    A(n,d) = Sym(V)^U,

bigraded by symmetric-power degree `m` and by `GL(d)` weight. Its weight
semigroup is

    S(n,d) = { (m, lambda) : mult(V_lambda, Sym^m(wedge^n C^d)) > 0 }
             subset of  Z_{>=0} x Z^d_{dom},

together with `0`. This is the preferred target verbatim: the normalized
support `{ lambda / m : (m,lambda) in S(n,d) }` has closure exactly the
fermionic moment polytope `P(n,d)`, which is how `src/gpc_census/plethysm.py`
and `scripts/plethysm_inner_hull.py` already certify inner points. The
combinatorial fallback target (facet-normal templates under transport) is NOT
computed here and no statement in this campaign mixes the two.

`S(n,d)` is a semigroup, and this is not assumed: `Sym(V)` is a domain, so its
subalgebra `A(n,d)` is a domain, and `A(n,d)` is graded by
`Z_{>=0} x Z^d` with `S(n,d)` its support; the product of two nonzero
homogeneous elements is nonzero and homogeneous of the sum degree, so the
support is closed under addition. `A(n,d)` is finitely generated
(Hadziev-Grosshans, `U`-invariants of a reductive group acting on an affine
variety), so `S(n,d)` is a finitely generated semigroup and a finite minimal
generating set exists at every `(n,d)`. The campaign measures HOW LARGE that
set is and whether it compresses in `d`.

## The valuations, and why each one is a valuation

Three genuinely different valuations. Each is scored separately and the winner
is not chosen on the training set.

**V2, the highest-weight valuation** (the brief's Valuation B, taken in its
"or equivalent highest-weight valuation" form). For `0 != f` in `A(n,d)`,
write `f = sum f_{m,lambda}` in the bigrading and set `v2(f)` to the
lexicographically largest `(m, lambda)` with `f_{m,lambda} != 0`. A group
grading of a domain by a totally ordered group makes the top-degree map
multiplicative, because the top component of a product is the product of the
top components and `A(n,d)` has no zero divisors. So `v2` is a valuation with
value semigroup exactly `S(n,d)`.

DECLARED LOSS, stated before the run because it is a candidate answer to Task
6: `v2` is NOT of maximal rank. The graded piece at `(m,lambda)` has dimension
`mult(V_lambda, Sym^m V)`, which the pilot already shows reaching 2 at
`(3,6)` degree 6, so `v2` collapses a multiplicity space to a single value and
its value semigroup cannot recover `A(n,d)`.

**V1, the Pluecker term order** (the brief's Valuation A). `Sym(V)` is the
polynomial ring on the Pluecker variables `p_S`, `S` an `n`-subset of `[d]`.
Order the variables by LEX on the increasing tuple `S` (so
`p_{123} > p_{124} > ... > p_{d-2,d-1,d}`) and take GRADED LEX on monomials.
`v1(f)` is the exponent vector of the leading monomial of `f`, in
`Z_{>=0}^{binom(d,n)}`. A term order on a polynomial ring gives a valuation on
every subalgebra that is a domain, so `v1` restricted to `A(n,d)` is a
valuation; its value semigroup is `S1(n,d) = v1(A(n,d) \ {0})`. This is the
literal SAGBI setting and a minimal generating set of `S1` is the value set of
a SAGBI (Khovanskii) basis of `A(n,d)` in the tested scope.

The variable order is STABLE UNDER RANK EMBEDDING: `C^d subset C^{d+1}` sends
`p_S` to `p_S` and lex on increasing tuples restricted to subsets of `[d]` is
the lex order of rank `d`, so the leading monomial of an element of
`A(n,d) subset A(n,d+1)` is the same in both rings.

**V3, the exterior colex order** (the brief's Valuation C, in its
"exterior-weight order" form). Same construction with the variables ordered by
COLEX on `S` (compare the largest element first: `p_{123} < p_{124} < p_{134}
< p_{234} < p_{125} < ...`) and GRADED REVERSE LEX on monomials. Colex on
`n`-subsets is the order in which the exterior algebra basis is generated by
adding one orbital at a time, so it is the constructor's primitive normal
order in the sense the brief asks for, and it is also stable under rank
embedding for the same reason: colex on subsets of `[d]` is an initial segment
of colex on subsets of `[d+1]`.

`v1` and `v3` are both valuations for the same one-line reason and both refine
`v2`: every monomial of a weight-homogeneous element carries the same weight,
so the leading exponent determines `(m, lambda)`. Their value semigroups
therefore surject onto `S(n,d)`, with fiber over `(m,lambda)` of size exactly
`mult(V_lambda, Sym^m V)`, which is the multiplicity information `v2` loses.

STATED AND NOT DONE: the Gelfand-Tsetlin or string valuation proper. The
obstruction is exact and is recorded here so that its absence is not read as
an oversight. A GT or string valuation is a valuation on the coordinate ring
of a FLAG variety or on an irreducible `GL(d)`-module, indexed by GT patterns
of a single highest weight. `A(n,d)` is not such a module: its
`(m,lambda)`-piece is a MULTIPLICITY space, on which `GL(d)` acts trivially,
so GT patterns do not index a basis of it. The honest GT construction values
the full ring `C[wedge^n C^d]` along a complete flag in `G/B`, which needs a
rank-`d(d-1)/2` valuation (15 at `d = 6`) computed as an order of vanishing
along a 15-step flag through a Borel-generic point. That is a symbolic
computation well outside the budget declared below, and no cheaper exact
implementation exists in this repository or in a permitted dependency. V1 and
V3 are the substitutes, as the brief allows.

A statistic that is not a valuation is not called one anywhere in this
campaign, and no result is stated for one.

## Budgets, declared in advance

Exactness: all arithmetic is exact integer or `Fraction`. No floating point
enters any scored number.

- V2 (cheap; the plethysm side): `(3,6)`, `(3,7)`, `(3,8)`, `(3,9)`, `(3,10)`
  and the cross-`N` systems `(4,8)`, `(4,10)`, `(5,10)`, each to the largest
  symmetric-power degree `m` reached inside a per-system wall budget of 1800
  seconds, with a hard cap of `m <= 18`. The reached `m` is recorded per system
  and a system that stops early is recorded as `operational`, never as
  mathematical evidence, per the methodology label already used in
  `docs/stage1_reference_series.md`.
- V1 and V3 (expensive; explicit highest-weight vectors): `(3,6)` and `(3,7)`
  to the largest `m` inside a per-system wall budget of 1800 seconds, with a
  hard cap of `m <= 8`. `(3,8)` is attempted at a hard cap of `m <= 5` and is
  allowed to fail operationally.
- No external algebra system. Normaliz is not used. Minimal generators are
  computed directly from the positively graded structure: an element of degree
  `m` is decomposable exactly when it is `a + b` with `a` of degree `j` and `b`
  of degree `m - j` for some `1 <= j <= m/2`, both in the semigroup, which is a
  finite exact integer search inside the computed scope. No Hilbert-basis
  library is required and none is added as a dependency.

## Holdout and its provenance (rule R2)

The rank direction is NOT independent across `d`. Padding `C^d subset C^{d+1}`
embeds `S(n,d)` into `S(n,d+1)` as the elements with `lambda_{d+1} = 0`, so
every generator inherited by padding is a DERIVED measurement in the sense of
R2 and confirms only the invariance of padding itself. Each scorecard entry
therefore reports:

- `scope`: all minimal generators in the tested window;
- `independent_scope`: PRIMITIVE generators only, those with `lambda_d > 0`,
  which cannot come from any lower rank by padding;
- `independent_distinct_states`: distinct primitive generators after
  particle-hole identification, where particle-hole applies.

The degree direction inside one rank IS independent: nothing in the
construction forces a degree-`m` element to be predictable from degree
`< m` data, and that is exactly what P5 tests.

## Power caveat, stated before scoring

A PASS on P1 would establish rank stabilization only within the declared
degree window and only for `n = 3` at `d <= 10`. It would NOT establish a
Khovanskii or SAGBI basis for `A(n,d)` at any `d`, would NOT be a statement
about all `d`, and would NOT bear on rank 20 or rank 40 except as a
conjecture. A FAIL with explicit new generators at every rank is the stronger
outcome here, because it forecloses a research direction with a witness rather
than with a budget.

## Predictions

P0 (GATE, forced, uninformative as evidence). For every `(n,d,m)` in the V1
and V3 scope, the dimension of the exactly computed space of highest-weight
vectors of weight `lambda` in `Sym^m(wedge^n C^d)`, obtained by kernel of the
simple raising operators over `Q`, equals the plethysm multiplicity computed by
`gpc_census.plethysm`. Expectation: PASS. Two independent routes to the same
integer; a mismatch means one of them is wrong and nothing else in this file
may be read. Recorded as a code gate, exactly as P1 was in
`docs/prereg_quantization_character.md`.

P1 (RANK STABILIZATION; the headline compression test). The number of
PRIMITIVE minimal generators of `S(3,d)` in the common degree window
`m <= M_common` is bounded independent of `d` over `d = 6..10`, in the strong
form: the count is non-increasing in `d` from `d = 7` on. Expectation: FAIL.
Rationale, committed before the run: `wedge^3 C^d` has finitely many `GL(d)`
orbits exactly for `d <= 8`, and carries moduli from `d = 9`; nothing in the
repository supplies an ordered-functor structure on the semi-invariant algebra
beyond padding; and the raw supports already grow with `d` at fixed `m`
(35, 65, 116 at `m = 8` for `d = 6, 7, 8`). A PASS would be a major positive
surprise and would be the first evidence for a rank-independent presentation.

P2 (DEGREE BOUND). The maximum degree of a minimal generator of `S(3,d)` in
the tested window is at most `d - 2`. Expectation: FAIL. Rationale: this is
the sharpest specific bound consistent with the pilot at `d = 6` (max
generator degree 4 through degree 4), and the campaign is worth running only
if it is stated sharply enough to break.

P3 (PADDING IS EXACT). `Gen(3,d) intersect {lambda_d = 0} = Gen(3,d-1)` as
sets, at every `d` in scope and every degree in the common window. Expectation:
PASS. This is a theorem, not a guess: coordinates are nonnegative, so a sum
with last coordinate 0 has both summands with last coordinate 0, and
minimality transfers in both directions. Recorded as a transport gate; a FAIL
means the code is wrong.

P4 (PARTICLE-HOLE). `S(3,6)` is invariant under
`lambda -> (m - lambda_6, ..., m - lambda_1)`, and `S(n,d)` maps onto
`S(d-n,d)` under the same complement at every system where both sides are in
scope. Expectation: PASS. This is the census's own transport, and it is a real
test of the plethysm code because the two sides are computed independently.

P5 (HELD-OUT DEGREE GENERATION; the preregistered held-out test, chosen
BEFORE fitting). Fit the minimal generators of degree `<= K` with
`K = ceil(M/2)` for each system, then predict degrees `K+1` and `K+2`. The
candidate basis PASSES only if BOTH hold: every element of `S_m` for
`m = K+1, K+2` is a nonnegative integer combination of the fitted generators
(no MISSES), and no nonnegative integer combination of the fitted generators
of total degree `m` lies outside `S_m` (no SPURIOUS predictions). Expectation:
FAIL on the miss side. Rationale: the pilot already shows two new generators
appearing at degree 4 at `(3,6)`, so new generators are not concentrated at
low degree.

P6 (HELD-OUT RANK; chosen BEFORE fitting). Fit at `d = 6` and `d = 7`, predict
`d = 8`: every minimal generator of `S(3,8)` in the common window is either a
padded generator of `S(3,7)` or lies in a family already present at `d = 7`
under the padding map. Expectation: FAIL, and the witnesses are the deliverable.

P7 (VALUATION COMPARISON). V1 and V3 produce the SAME number of minimal
generators of their value semigroups in the common scope. Expectation: FAIL.
Rationale: SAGBI bases are order dependent; equality would be evidence that the
Pluecker leading-term structure is order independent here, which nothing
predicts.

P8 (FINER IS NOT SMALLER). Neither V1 nor V3 achieves a compression ratio
(minimal generators divided by raw values) better than V2 in the common scope.
Expectation: PASS, and it is close to forced by the fiber count, so it is
recorded at low evidential weight. It is stated because the brief requires the
valuations to be compared on a fixed table rather than by narrative.

P9 (SATURATION FAILS, AND IT IS THE QUANTIZATION PERIOD). For every census
vertex `lambda/q` of `(3,6)` and `(3,7)`, the set of `m` with `(m, m*lambda/q)`
in `S(3,d)` is exactly the set of multiples of the quantization period `p`
recorded in `results/data/quantization_multiplicities.json`, and in particular
`S(3,6)` is NOT saturated in its own cone because
`(2, (1,1,1,1,1,1))` is a lattice point of the cone that is absent from the
semigroup. Expectation: PASS. This is a cross-script consistency check between
two campaigns that share no code path beyond the plethysm primitives, and a
FAIL invalidates one of them.

## Scoring rules

PASS / FAIL / UNINFORMATIVE / ALREADY-FALSIFIED per prediction, decided by the
artifact alone. Each verdict reports scope, independent scope, and independent
distinct states. No prediction is edited after the run. Predictions whose scope
is empty by construction are UNINFORMATIVE, never PASS (rule R3).

## Abort criterion, declared in advance

The valuation is abandoned after the scope above, with no budget extension, if
either holds:

- the primitive generator count tracks the raw value count with no compression
  signal, meaning the ratio of primitive generators to raw values fails to fall
  as `d` increases; or
- held-out failure is systematic rather than a repairable convention bug,
  meaning misses appear at more than one rank and are not removed by any fixed
  relabeling of the value coordinates.

The budget is not expanded to postpone a negative conclusion. Resource
exhaustion is recorded as `operational` and is never reported as mathematical
evidence.
